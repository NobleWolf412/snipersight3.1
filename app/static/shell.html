How the scanner works

the five steps these rules control

It is always running. The scanner re-checks every symbol it watches, around the clock. Run Scan on Command does not do anything extra — it runs the same check straight away instead of waiting for the next one.

  1. Pick what to watch. The most heavily traded symbols on the venue are admitted and thin markets are left out — a stop in a thin book does not get filled where you put it. Controlled by: how many symbols to admit, minimum 24h volume, trade perpetual futures.
  2. Learn each market before trading it. A new symbol is watched but never sized until there is enough history to map its levels honestly — about six months. A market whose structure the engine cannot see is one it will not risk money in.
  3. Read the conditions. For each symbol it works out the trend, marks the supply and demand zones, and notes where liquidity is resting. This is what Market Weather shows: what state each market is in right now.
  4. Wait for price to come to you. A tradeable condition is not a trade. A setup appears only when price returns to one of those zones and confirms there — then it lands on the Setup Deck. Controlled by: the playbook switches above.
  5. Check it against your limits. Even a valid setup is refused if it would break a risk rule — too many trades open, too much at stake, or resting after a recent stop-out. Command says which rule stopped it. Controlled by: the risk settings below.

So an empty Setup Deck beside a busy Market Weather is the normal state, not a fault. Weather is necessary and nowhere near sufficient: a symbol can sit in a tradeable regime for days without price ever coming back to a zone. Quiet days are the design.

Nothing here places a real order. Arm writes to your own paper book, kept separate from the strategy's record so hand-picked trades are never counted as engine edge. Live execution is locked.

Venue & Credentials

—

Keys are encrypted with Windows DPAPI, tied to your user account. They are never written to the fact store, the log, or git, and no endpoint can read one back — the app can only tell you whether one is set. Use read-only keys. A stored key does not enable live trading; that gate is separate and still locked.

Risk

These are the live numbers the risk authority sizes with, read from the engine. They are not editable here yet — and not because the field is missing. Changing risk per trade re-sizes the entire forward record, because the account is recomputed by replaying every setup. Doing that in place would rewrite your paper track record under the same engine version, which is exactly what the versioning rule forbids. The envelope is also coupled: the total cap holds two concurrent trades and the daily halt is about three stop-outs, so one value cannot move alone.
